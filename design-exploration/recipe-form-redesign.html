What the redesign is doing.

The visual brief is editorial — set the recipe like it belongs in a printed book — but every change was scored against a usefulness bar. Nothing decorative made the cut on its own.

01 Live per‑ingredient macros
The proposed feature shipped here as a small text-xs sub-line beneath the name/amount row. Updates synchronously with the amount, piece count, and grams-per-piece inputs — no debounce, since the editor is fully controlled by the parent form. Untracked rows stay silent.
02 Recipe title as display type
Fraunces italic at opsz 96 with a serif accent on connectors like alla. The name carries the screen instead of the toolbar.
03 Totals strip becomes a focal point
The big 412 in Fraunces italic anchors the eye. Total-for-all-servings demotes to a secondary line with a dashed rule — present, but quiet.
04 Untracked rows look untracked
A subtle warm tint runs edge-to-edge. The macro sub-line is gone, the toggle pill flips to filled-ink, and the quantity reads as "1 Prise" in italic serif — the same display-quantity affordance, with more typographic dignity.
05 Stepper for portions
Replaces the bare number input with a pill-stepper. Cheaper to tap on mobile, and the macro/kcal totals upstairs animate with it for free.
06 AI‑import has identity
A dark ink banner ("AUS 3 FOTOS EXTRAHIERT") plus a thumbnail strip. Unmatched ingredients sit in a warm amber card directly above the form, never beneath — they're a precondition, not an afterthought.
07 Save as primary affordance
Promoted to a small accent pill in the top bar. Visible without scrolling on long recipes; mirrors the in-app pattern already used in the daily log.
08 Restraint, not noise
One accent (forkcast indigo, kept), one warm neutral palette, two typefaces, one paper texture. Everything else earns its way in by being functional first.
